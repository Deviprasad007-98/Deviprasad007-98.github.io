Attosecond photonics

Field and Time-resolved Attosecond spectroscopy by TIPTOE field sampling optical system

I sample the electric field of light directly with a custom TIPTOE setup driven by a sub-5.5 fs, CEP-stable Ti:sapphire oscillator, for attosecond spectroscopy of III–V semiconductors and petahertz photonics.

How it works. A strong few-cycle drive pulse ionizes a medium only within a sub-cycle window at its peak. A weak signal field placed at delay τ slightly changes the ionization yield. Scanning τ makes the yield modulation δN/N trace out the signal's electric field, Es(τ).

Ultrafast optics & 2D materials

Femtosecond transient-absorption spectroscopy of 2D MXenes with a wavelength-tunable pump

At IIT Madras I built a custom pump–probe transient-absorption setup for 2D MXenes, driven by a 30 fs CPA Ti:sapphire laser whose 800, 400 (SHG) and 266 nm (THG) outputs are separated by a prism and selected with a tilting mirror, and used the same laser for high-harmonic generation in an ultra-high-vacuum beamline.

How it works. A pump pulse excites carriers in the sample. A delayed broadband probe measures the change in absorbance, ΔA(λ, τ). Scanning the delay τ with a motorised stage follows how the hot carriers cool and relax. The transient signal shows ground-state bleaching (ΔA < 0) and photo-induced absorption (ΔA > 0).

Energy-harvesting devices

Triboelectric/Piezoelectric nanogenerators for self-powered sensing

At CIPET I fabricated and COMSOL-simulated TENG/PENG energy-harvesting devices from electrospun PVDF nanofibres with 2D nanofillers (MXene, hBN, rGO), reaching 80 V and 22 mW m−2 with PVDF-MXene vs PDMS, and turned them into self-powered sensors for health monitoring such as real-time wrist-pulse tracking.

How it works. When the two layers touch, contact electrification leaves the PVDF-nanofibre surface negative and the counter layer positive. As they separate, electrostatic induction pushes electrons through the external load; pressing them back together reverses the current.

Non-linear optics semiconductor thin films

Energy-treatment engineering of As–Se–Ge/Sb/Te chalcogenide thin films

ThesisStructural, morphological and optical study of doped As–Se films under different energy treatments

I prepared Ge-, Sb- and Te-doped As–Se films by melt quenching and thermal evaporation, modified them by thermal annealing, laser irradiation and ion irradiation, and characterised them with XRD, Raman, FESEM, AFM, EDAX, XPS and UV-Vis-NIR spectroscopy. The nonlinear optical parameters (χ(3) and n2) shifted along with the bandgap and refractive index, so each treatment gave me a practical way to tune the films' nonlinear response after deposition, and I later extended this to Ag(Te)/As–Se and Te/As2Se3 bilayers.
