      Attosecond photonics

      Field and Time-resolved Attosecond spectroscopy by TIPTOE field sampling optical system

      I use a custom TIPTOE setup (Tunnelling Ionization with Perturbation for the Time-domain Observation of an Electric field) to sample the electric field of light directly. It is driven by a sub-5.5 fs, CEP-stable Ti:sapphire oscillator (800 nm, 80 MHz). I also work on attosecond spectroscopy using tunnelling ionization in III–V semiconductors, and on Z-scan, chirp correction and CEP-dependent measurements for petahertz photonics.

      How it works. A strong few-cycle drive pulse ionizes a medium only within a sub-cycle window at its peak. A weak signal field placed at delay τ slightly changes the ionization yield. Scanning τ makes the yield modulation δN/N trace out the signal's electric field, Es(τ).

      Ultrafast optics & 2D materials

      Femtosecond transient-absorption spectroscopy of 2D MXenes with a wavelength-tunable pump

      At the Femtosecond Photonics lab, IIT Madras (Oct 2025 – Mar 2026), I developed a custom pump–probe setup for transient-absorption spectroscopy to characterise 2D MXenes. The source is a 30 fs CPA Ti:sapphire laser at 800 nm. The pump wavelength is tuned by harmonic generation: an SHG crystal converts 800 nm to 400 nm, and a THG stage gives ~266 nm. A prism then separates the 800, 400 and 266 nm beams, and tilting a mirror selects which one passes through an iris to pump the sample with 1.55, 3.1 or 4.65 eV photons. I also performed high-harmonic generation experiments in an ultra-high-vacuum beamline driven by the same laser.

      How it works. A pump pulse excites carriers in the sample. A delayed broadband probe measures the change in absorbance, ΔA(λ, τ). Scanning the delay τ with a motorised stage follows how the hot carriers cool and relax. The transient signal shows ground-state bleaching (ΔA < 0) and photo-induced absorption (ΔA > 0).

      Energy-harvesting devices

      Triboelectric/Piezoelectric nanogenerators for self-powered sensing

      At CIPET (Dec 2023 – Oct 2025) I electrospun PVDF nanofibres loaded with three 2D nanofillers: MXene (Ti3C2Tx, by HF etching), hBN nanosheets (by liquid-phase exfoliation) and rGO. I modelled the device in COMSOL and built a 3D-printed contact-separation TENG driven by a vibrational shaker. The best pair, PVDF-MXene/ITO-PET vs PDMS, reached 80 V, 250 nA cm−2, 5 nC cm−2 and 22 mW m−2 at 6 MΩ, and stayed stable for ~16,000 cycles. The same materials made a self-powered pressure sensor (0.5 → 8 nA cm−2 from 0.1 to 0.5 N) and a wrist-pulse monitor that responds to deep breathing (2 nA cm−2) and running (14 nA cm−2).

      How it works. When the two layers touch, contact electrification leaves the PVDF-nanofibre surface negative and the counter layer positive. As they separate, electrostatic induction pushes electrons through the external load; pressing them back together reverses the current. MXene gives the composite the highest dielectric constant (38.1 at 100 Hz, vs 12.7 with hBN and 8.1 with rGO) and the strongest β-phase (d33 ≈ 3.2 pC N−1), so more charge is induced.

      Non-linear optics semiconductor thin films

      Energy-treatment engineering of As–Se–Ge/Sb/Te chalcogenide thin films

      ThesisStructural, morphological and optical study of doped As–Se films under different energy treatments

      I prepared As–Se glasses doped with Ge, Sb or Te by melt quenching and deposited them as thin films by thermal evaporation. I then modified the films with three energy routes: thermal annealing, laser irradiation and ion irradiation. I followed the structural, morphological and optical changes with XRD, Raman, FESEM, AFM, EDAX, XPS and UV-Vis-NIR spectroscopy, and extracted the linear and nonlinear optical parameters. Because the third-order susceptibility χ(3) and nonlinear refractive index n2 are closely linked to the bandgap and linear refractive index, every energy treatment (annealing below/above Tg, laser irradiation tracked in situ, and fluence-dependent ion irradiation), together with Ag(Te)/As–Se and Te/As2Se3 bilayers, acts as a post-deposition control knob for the nonlinear optics of As–Se–Ge/Sb/Te films.

      How it works. As-deposited chalcogenide films are amorphous: a disordered network of As, Se and dopant atoms. Each energy treatment rearranges that network in a different way. Heat lets it relax and, at high enough temperature, crystallise. Light switches bonds (photo-induced changes). Ions break bonds and create defects. These changes shift the absorption edge, optical bandgap and refractive index, which is how the optical response can be tuned after deposition.
